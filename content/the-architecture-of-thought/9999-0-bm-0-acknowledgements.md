This inquiry stands upon the shoulders of thinkers who refused to accept names as explanations, who insisted on inspecting the gears of reality with their own hands.

Deep gratitude to the pioneers whose empirical discoveries and mathematical formulations provided the bedrock for these thirty-six chapters:

- **Richard Feynman**, for teaching us that if you cannot explain an idea from simple primitives to a child without jargon, you do not truly understand it.
- **Claude Shannon and Rolf Landauer**, for proving that information is not an ethereal spirit, but a physical quantity inextricably bound to thermodynamic entropy.
- **Alan Turing and Kurt Gödel**, for mapping the profound, permanent mathematical boundaries of formal logic and mechanical computation.
- **Karl Friston**, for the unifying mathematics of the Free Energy Principle and predictive processing.
- **Daniel Kahneman, Amos Tversky, and Herbert Simon**, for dismantling the myth of homo economicus and revealing the heuristic architecture of human decision.
- **Antonio Damasio**, for demonstrating that without the somatic markers of emotion, reason cannot take a single step into the world.
- **Michael Gazzaniga and Roger Sperry**, for exposing the left-hemisphere interpreter and the fictional mechanics of the narrative ego.
- **John O'Keefe, Edvard Moser, and May-Britt Moser**, for discovering the crystalline hexagonal coordinates of the brain’s internal GPS.
- **Edmund Husserl and Maurice Merleau-Ponty**, for the rigorous courage of the phenomenological stance: reminding us that every scientific dial must ultimately be witnessed by a conscious eye.
- **David Chalmers**, for holding the Hard Problem of Consciousness open against premature, simplistic reductions.

Finally, to the open-source software contributors, typographers, and digital craftspeople whose work made the *folio* reading engine possible—proving that static, sovereign, distraction-free reading can thrive on the open web.
